The problem

Every piece of meat has a story. Most of it gets lost along the way.

An animal raised in a specific place. A producer who knows every detail of where it came from. A chain of decisions, care, and time.

But by the time meat reaches the counter or the plate, that story is already gone. What's left is just a product, no face, no place, no journey.

What if that story didn't have to disappear?

The solution

One scan. The story behind the product.

Meat Azores links each cut of meat to a digital experience. A simple scan gives consumers real information about the product's origin and journey, producer, breed, farm, territory, and point of sale.

The QR code isn't the product. It's just the way in.

How it works

From farm to plate, without losing the thread.

Meat Azores organizes and connects the information that matters along the chain, and makes it accessible to whoever reaches the end of it.

Vision

What if we told more stories from the Azores?

We started with beef. What if we also told the stories behind other Azorean products and the families who have made them for generations?

We want Azorean products to be better known at home and abroad, and their producers to have a voice in the markets where they are sold. Knowing where these products come from, how they are made and who makes them helps us understand what sets them apart.

Beyond the Azores

Born in the Azores. Built to go further.

The thinking behind Meat Azores, identity, origin, accessible information, isn't limited to one product or one region. It can adapt to PDO and PGI products, premium food supply chains, and territories with a strong origin identity to protect.

About

Where design and technology meet origin.

Meat Azores was born from the desire to apply design, technology, and digital experience to a real problem: making the origin and value behind Azorean products more accessible. Its founder brings a background in design and technology to a territory where identity, production, and origin are deeply connected.

None of this would exist without Talho Rocha (opens in a new tab), a family butcher in Agualva, on Terceira Island, with 36 years behind the counter. The idea was born from its daily contact with restaurants and customers: the butcher knew the breed and age of every animal, but that information didn't always reach the table.
